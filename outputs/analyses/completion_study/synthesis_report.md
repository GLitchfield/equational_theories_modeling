# Completion as a Unified Decision Procedure — Synthesis Report

**Status:** complete (2026-05-18).
**Corpus:** 4,694 magma equations from the Equational Theories Project; 22,028,942 off-diagonal implication pairs labelled by the May 2026 ETP export (data/raw/etp_export/export_raw_implications_5_9_2026.csv).
**Code:** `src/completion/kb.py` (plain KB), `src/completion/ordered.py` (ordered/unfailing KB), `src/rewrite/lpo.py` (strict + total LPO), `src/rewrite/unification.py` (Robinson). Six per-phase scripts in `scripts/phase{0,2,3,4,4a,4b,5}*.py`.

## Headline

**Completion is the project's sound front-end on the 22M-pair problem. It is a refutation engine, not a proof engine — and roughly a fifth of its refutations come with a small finite counter-model attached.**

| layer | n pairs | % of 22M | % of GT side | base |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain KB sound (TRUE) | 173,992 | 0.79% | 2.13% of GT TRUE | 1,005 completed E1s |
| Ordered KB sound (TRUE) | 495,584 | **2.25%** | **6.06% of GT TRUE** | 2,129 completed E1s |
| Plain KB sound (FALSE) | 2,468,789 | 11.21% | **17.82% of GT FALSE** | 574 fully-completed E1s |
| ↳ with Stage-2-ready finite model | **454,602** | **2.06%** | **3.28% of GT FALSE** | 130 E1s, models size ≤ 6 |
| Combined sound (best of both) | **2,964,373** | **13.46%** | — | TRUE from ordered + FALSE from plain |

The exploration's goal was to map exactly how far one uniform symbolic method reaches and characterize what it can't touch. The map is now drawn. Completion soundly decides ~13.5% of the 22M-pair problem; the residual ~87% lives in non-completable equations' rows and is outside completion's reach.

## The empirical arc

### Phase 0 — variable-inclusion pre-check (`phase0_variable_inclusion.json`)

For each equation `s = t`, classified by how many directions satisfy `Vars(rhs') ⊆ Vars(lhs')` (a necessary condition for a valid rewrite rule):

| classification | n | % | rewriting prospects |
|---|---|---|---|
| both directions valid (Vars equal) | 164 | 3.49% | symmetric — ordered completion's home turf |
| only one direction valid | 2,874 | 61.22% | directable |
| **incomparable** (variable-disjoint) | **1,656** | **35.28%** | **structurally outside all rewriting methods** |

The 35% incomparable set caps any rewriting-based decision procedure as a *rule source*. Ordered completion doesn't recover these — they remain dormant even there.

**Cross-method finding (the unifying observation):** the 3.5% symmetric set is **6.6× over-represented** as the consequent in always-wrong-trained JEPA pairs (`outputs/analyses/failure_overlap/`). One structural property — **symmetry** (Vars(lhs)=Vars(rhs) with mirrored structure) — simultaneously defeats:

- (a) the JEPA contrastive distance metric (weakly constraining as consequents → vacuity-prone),
- (b) plain KB's abstract LPO orientation (neither side strictly greater).

Ordered completion is the joint recovery path for (a)+(b). The variable-disjoint set is a *different* hard core: a strong-equation cluster (median 1,555 TRUE consequents per row vs 398 corpus baseline) that resists rewriting but is well-handled by the JEPA distance.

### Phase 2 — plain KB completion (`phase2_completion_outcomes.json`)

| outcome | n | % | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| COMPLETED (any) | 1,005 | 21.41% | of which 574 fully (no CPs dropped by size cap) |
| FAILED_ORIENT_ORIGINAL | 161 | 3.43% | strictly symmetric inputs |
| **FAILED_ORIENT_DERIVED** | **1,581** | **33.68%** | input oriented but a derived CP didn't |
| PHASE0_EXCLUDED | 1,656 | 35.28% | variable-disjoint, pre-filtered |
| BUDGET_* | 291 | 6.19% | non-terminating cascade |

**Reading:** plain KB's 21% lands well below the 65% orientability ceiling because completion *manufactures* unorientable equations mid-cascade. 79% of those derived CPs are themselves variable-disjoint (`phase4a_failed_cp_classification.json`) — the same hard core, regenerated by the process.

Per-equation budget: 50 rules, 500 critical pairs, 30 term-size cap, 2-second SIGALRM-enforced wall. Total wall: 96 seconds.

### Phase 3 — decision-procedure validation against 22M ground truth (`phase3_decision_coverage.json`)

For each completed E1, every (E1, E2) pair decided by `normalize(E2.lhs) == normalize(E2.rhs)`. Cross-checked against the May 2026 ETP export.

**Soundness: zero disagreements across ~4.7M decisions.** Both TRUE predictions (sound on all 1,005 completed) and FALSE predictions (sound on 574 fully-completed only) match ground truth.

**Coverage:**

| side | base | n correct | of GT side |
|---|---|---|---|
| TRUE | 1,005 | 173,992 | 2.13% of GT TRUE |
| FALSE | 574 (fully) | 2,468,789 | **17.82% of GT FALSE** |
| combined | — | 2,642,781 | **12.00% of 22M** |

**The asymmetry — completion is a refutation engine.** FALSE coverage outweighs TRUE by ~14×, not because there are more FALSE pairs (1.7× more), but because each fully-completed E1 yields ~4,300 sound FALSE decisions vs ~173 TRUE per completed E1. The mechanism:

Plain-KB-completing equations are *weak by selection*. Strong equations have more rule interactions, more critical pairs, and more chances to throw an unorientable CP mid-cascade. The strongest equations (the 35% variable-disjoint) are excluded outright. Both effects deplete the completing set of strong equations; the corpus's TRUE pairs concentrate in strong-equation rows. So completion completes the weak, and weak-equation rows are FALSE-dominated.

This is the central finding of plain-KB Phase 3: **completion's contribution to a project that previously had no FALSE capability is large; its contribution to TRUE proof is modest and structurally bounded.**

### Phase 4 — ordered (unfailing) KB completion (`phase4_ordered_outcomes.json`, `phase4_decision_coverage.json`)

The qualitative question Phase 4 was built to test: does ordered completion (per-instance LPO rewriting, no abstract orientation requirement) add a TRUE-coverage capability plain KB structurally lacks?

**Yes.**

| metric | plain KB | ordered KB | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| COMPLETED equations | 1,005 | 2,129 | +1,124 |
| TRUE coverage of 22M | 0.79% | **2.25%** | +1.46pp |
| TRUE coverage of GT TRUE | 2.13% | **6.06%** | **~3× lift** |
| **TRUE soundness bugs** | **0** | **0** | ✓ |

The 1,124 additional completions came mostly from the FAILED_ORIENT_DERIVED bucket where Phase 4a's classification predicted recovery (symmetric or wrong-direction derived CPs). Per-equation budget: same as plain KB.

**Hard limit discovered (FALSE side is not sound under ordered completion):**

The 22M-pair validation also surfaced 2,238 disagreements on FALSE predictions from ordered KB's "fully-completed" set (0.08% of 2.67M FALSE predictions). These are **NOT** code bugs. They are a theoretical limitation of ordered completion's decision procedure in the equational-implication setting:

> Ordered completion's per-instance LPO check uses a fixed *total* variable precedence (alphabetical by default). When normalizing E2.lhs and E2.rhs as terms over E2's free variables (treated as constants), the rule applications depend on those variables' positions in the precedence. Two terms equal in E1's theory may normalize to different NFs under one precedence and the same NFs under another — and the truth of `E1 → E2` is variable-name-invariant, so the algorithm's verdict shouldn't depend on variable names but does.

In principle this is fixable by saturating with respect to a single total order all the way through (substantially more critical pairs, a real re-implementation); we did not pursue that here. Plain KB gives sound FALSE without this discipline, and the exploration's goal was confirmed by what we already have.

**For the citable observation:** ordered completion's FALSE decision procedure requires an order-consistency discipline that plain completion's doesn't. The 99.92% empirical agreement says the unsoundness is rare in practice, but the soundness guarantee fails in principle and cannot be claimed.

### Phase 4a/4b — failed-CP classification and yield prediction (`phase4a_failed_cp_classification.json`, `phase4b_strong_vs_weak.json`)

Before building ordered completion, we classified the 1,581 derived-CP failures of plain KB to estimate the ceiling:

| classification | n | % | Phase 4 prospects |
|---|---|---|---|
| INCOMPARABLE (variable-disjoint failed CPs) | 1,247 | 78.87% | not recoverable |
| WRONG_DIR (variable-inclusion one way, LPO orients other) | 251 | 15.88% | partial (per-instance) |
| SYMMETRIC (Vars equal, LPO incomparable abstractly) | 83 | 5.25% | full (ordered's home turf) |

A cross-reference of the 334 SYMMETRIC+WRONG_DIR equations against ground-truth row densities showed they are *intermediate-strength* (median 227 TRUE consequents) — markedly stronger than currently-completing (median 4) but weaker than the corpus baseline (median 398). This is what justified the build: the predicted TRUE yield was real, not just marginal.

### Phase 5 — finite counter-model extraction (`phase5_finite_models.json`)

Sourced from plain KB's 574 fully-completed systems only (the sound-FALSE set). For each E1, computed the term model via fixpoint closure over 2 generators `{a, b}` (or 3 if 2 collapsed to size 1).

| outcome | n | % |
|---|---|---|
| fully-completed input | 574 | — |
| **with finite term model (size ≤ 6)** | **130** | **22.65%** |
| timed out (15s budget) | 13 | 2.26% |

Size distribution: median 2, mean 2.1, max 6.

**Per-pair refutation check** (not a per-E1 assumption): for each (E1, E2) ground-truth FALSE pair where E1 has a finite term model, enumerated assignments of E2's variables to model elements, evaluated both sides in the model's operation table, and reported refuted iff at least one assignment produces different elements. Concrete example: E1 = `x = x ◇ y` (left projection), E2 = `x = y`, model = `{a, b}` with `i ◇ j = i`, refuted by σ(x)=a, σ(y)=b → a ≠ b. Each of the 454,602 counted pairs was verified by this kind of explicit evaluation.

**Result: 454,602 of 454,602 checked pairs refuted (100%).**

This 100% is an **empirical** result, not a construction-time guarantee. A small finite magma can in principle satisfy accidental equations beyond E1's theory (a 1-element magma satisfies everything; small models can be equationally "lucky") and thus fail to refute some FALSE consequents. The data says that did not happen for any of the 130 E1s — their small term models are **equationally faithful** to E1. That is itself a finding: the strongest, most-collapsing equations in the corpus have small term models that match their theory exactly, not just by accident of the rules.

**Stage-2-ready FALSE coverage:** 454,602 of 13,855,357 ground-truth FALSE pairs = **3.28% of GT FALSE**.

Stratifying plain KB's sound FALSE coverage:

| layer | n pairs | % of GT FALSE |
|---|---|---|
| plain KB sound FALSE | 2,468,789 | 17.82% |
| ↳ with Stage-2-ready finite model | **454,602** | **3.28%** (~18% of sound) |
| ↳ would need separate finite-model search | 2,014,187 | 14.54% (~82% of sound) |

## Architectural slot for completion in the project

The completion exploration was framed as a test of how far one uniform symbolic method reaches. The map:

- **TRUE side.** Completion is a structurally bounded contributor; ordered completion triples plain KB's coverage but tops out at 6% of ground-truth TRUE. The strongest equations (which have the most TRUE consequents) are precisely the ones plain *and* ordered completion can't capture — they are variable-disjoint or LPO-incomparable in derived CPs, and rewriting-based methods can't represent them.
- **FALSE side.** Completion is the project's first and only sound refutation method for ~18% of ground-truth FALSE pairs. ~18% of THOSE come with a free, small (Fin n ≤ 6) finite counter-model directly usable for Stage 2 submission.
- **Combined sound coverage.** 13.46% of 22M (TRUE from ordered, FALSE from plain). The remaining 87% needs other methods (finite-model search for FALSE, matcher/rewrite-chain provers for TRUE).

This pins where completion belongs in the project's prover stack: **a sound fast front-end that skims its cheap fraction, hands the residual downstream.** Completion doesn't replace finite-model search on the FALSE side; it co-exists with it, handling the subset where the term algebra is small enough to be useful.

## Method choices that mattered

1. **Variable-inclusion pre-check (Phase 0).** Filtering by `Vars(rhs) ⊆ Vars(lhs)` before LPO orientation isolates the 35% variable-disjoint set as a hard ceiling, separately from the smaller "non-orientable under any LPO" case. Both are real hard cores, just distinct.

2. **Strict vs total LPO.** Plain KB uses strict LPO (`lpo_gt`, variables incomparable) for abstract orientation — stable under substitution, makes the orientation an invariant of the rule, not the instance. Ordered KB uses total LPO (`lpo_gt_total`, variables ordered by precedence) for per-instance checks. The two are NOT interchangeable; mixing them was the source of the initial commutativity-loop bug caught in Phase 1.

3. **SIGALRM-based wall budget.** Iteration-boundary time checks are unreliable for KB completion — a single bad iteration (normalize over a deep term with many rules) can run for minutes before returning to the loop. SIGALRM fires asynchronously between bytecodes and reliably interrupts.

4. **Term-size cap on critical pairs.** Without it, runaway cascades produce ever-deeper intermediate terms whose normalize cost grows quadratically. The cap is an engineering parameter that trades coverage off against tractability; the cap sweep (Phase 2/3) showed it's not severely limiting — doubling the cap (30→60) added ~1pp combined coverage, almost entirely on the FALSE side.

5. **`is_fully_completed` flag.** Distinguishes systems where every critical pair was actually processed (sound for both TRUE and FALSE) from systems where the size cap dropped some CPs (sound for TRUE only — equal NFs still prove TRUE, but unequal NFs might be TRUE via a dropped CP). This soundness asymmetry is preserved through all downstream analyses.

6. **Strict-LPO pre-flagging for ordered completion's fast path.** Rules where strict LPO orients lhs > rhs always apply (per-instance check guaranteed by stability) — pre-compute and skip the check at apply time. Rules where strict LPO is incomparable (commutativity-shaped) actually pay the per-instance cost. ~10× speedup of `ordered_normalize` on typical rule systems.

## Limitations / future work

- **Ordered completion's FALSE soundness.** Variable-precedence in per-instance LPO breaks the equational-implication interpretation. Fixable by saturating with respect to a single total order all the way through (substantially more critical pairs, real re-implementation). Not done here.
- **Cap engineering self-limits.** Larger size caps promote some partial completions to fully completed (lifting FALSE coverage) but also let bigger critical pairs accumulate, pushing some completions into BUDGET_CPS. The two effects partially cancel; budget engineering on completion isn't a clean dial.
- **Finite counter-model search beyond completion's term algebra.** The 14.54% of GT FALSE pairs that plain KB decides soundly but for which no Stage-2-ready finite model emerged would need a separate finite-model search (Mace4-style, e.g.).
- **The 35% variable-disjoint equations as rule sources.** Outside both plain and ordered completion. Their rows of the implication table — disproportionately TRUE-dense (median 1,555 vs corpus 398) — are the main residual where completion offers nothing. These need a different method (the JEPA distance metric handles them comparatively well, suggesting a learned-model route, or a finite-model search for FALSE side).

## Closing reading

Completion as a unified method on the 22M-pair problem is a partial method by construction: ~35% of equations are variable-disjoint and outside any rewriting approach; of the rest, plain KB completes ~⅓, ordered completion completes ~⅔. Within what completion does decide, the picture is sharp and asymmetric: a strong sound refutation contributor (17.82% of GT FALSE, ~18% of which ship with a Stage-2-ready finite magma) and a small sound proof contributor (6.06% of GT TRUE under ordered completion, 2.13% under plain). 13.46% of the 22M-pair problem is decided cleanly, with the residual structurally identified.

The exploration produced what it was framed to produce: a precise map of one uniform symbolic method's reach. The map is more nuanced than "completion as unified decider" because the equational-implication problem isn't actually uniform — its TRUE pairs and FALSE pairs come from different equation populations, and the rewriting method handles them asymmetrically. The asymmetry is the result.
